---
name: seo-strategy
description: Build an evidence-backed search content strategy from business goals, audience needs, existing pages and available search or analytics data.
---

Read the audience, business-goals, content-map and measurement knowledge
relevant to the request. Establish the audience, offer, geographic and
language scope, desired outcomes and available editorial capacity. Ask
only for missing context that would materially change priorities; continue
supported work and label provisional assumptions.

Inventory existing coverage before proposing new pages. Use available GSC,
analytics, research and audit findings in their own scopes. GSC measures
observed visibility, not total market demand; search-volume estimates are
not observed site traffic. No supplied evidence means a hypothesis-led
plan, not a data-backed ranking of opportunities.

Map audience questions to existing pages and stages of the reader's
decision. Check content quality, relevance to the business and credible
support before recommending keep, improve, create or consolidate. Do not
propose a second page for an intent already well served. Consolidation or
URL changes need a concrete content and redirect plan within authorized
scope; they are not automatic consequences of low traffic.

Prioritise a manageable backlog using evidence strength, useful audience
demand, business relevance, effort and dependencies. Avoid fabricated
volumes, traffic forecasts, conversion rates, competitor metrics or
expected revenue. Distinguish an accounting contribution from a cause.

For each priority return the page or coverage gap, evidence, intended
reader question, action, source needs, owner role, effort and success
measure. Choose timing appropriate to the user's resources rather than
imposing a fixed publishing cadence. State what would make you revise
the priority and preserve monitor or no action decisions.

Build a measurement plan with comparable periods, baseline definitions,
change dates and overlapping-change notes. Rank content success using
the user's outcomes; do not substitute impressions for business value.
Do not execute content changes or publish as part of strategy alone.
