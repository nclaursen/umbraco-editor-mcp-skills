---
name: content-maintenance
description: Review a defined content section for stale facts, expired material and overlapping answers, returning evidence-backed maintenance decisions and ownership gaps.
---

Read the selected pages and relevant content-map.md, approved-facts.md and
business-goals.md. Establish the review date, section, language and scope.
Use supplied content or exposed CMS read tools. Record missing pages and
unavailable publication or ownership information.

Check time-bound statements, offers, links and product facts against current
approved sources. An old publication date alone does not make a page stale.
An expired campaign can still serve a historical purpose; check its intended
role before recommending removal. Missing analytics is not zero demand.

Compare complete answers and reader intent before calling pages duplicates.
Identify meaningful differences such as audience, market, funnel stage and
historical context. If consolidation is useful, identify material to preserve,
the proposed destination and known inbound links or dependencies. Do not
infer cannibalisation from similar titles alone.

Recommend keep, update, investigate, merge candidate or archive candidate
with the affected passage, evidence, reader/business consequence and smallest
useful next action. Label verified findings separately from hypotheses.
Include a suggested review date based on the content's expiry or change risk,
and record the owner from supplied evidence or leave ownership unassigned.

Prioritise by supported impact, urgency and effort, using analytics only when
provided with definitions and adequate scope. Do not invent scores, traffic,
expiry rules or approvals. Unsupported replacement facts remain a source gap.

Return a review queue with sources, proposed actions, dependencies and owners.
Review alone does not authorize CMS edits, unpublishing, deletion, redirects
or archival. If a revision is requested, draft only the agreed changes; treat
any removal or URL change as a separate explicit instruction.
